title: The Door Between Worlds
position: 2

The connection took four hours to establish. Four hours of ARIA and me working in silence, bypassing safety protocols, draining power from nonessential systems, and essentially turning Horizon Station into a giant quantum transmitter.

When the green light finally flashed on the console, I was so tired I almost missed it.

"Connection established," ARIA said softly. "Maya—we have a link."

I sat at the communication console, my hands hovering over the controls. "Lila? Can you hear me?"

Static hissed from the speakers—then cleared. My sister's voice, tired but unmistakable.

*"Maya. You received my message."*

"I did. Are you—where are you? What happened?"

*"I'm not sure I can explain. The other side of the barrier—it's not a place. It's between places. Between moments, between choices. We're not dead, Maya. But we're not alive in the way you'd understand it."*

"The experiment—"

*"It worked. We made contact. Something came through—something curious, not hostile. It wanted to know about our side. We showed it everything. But then Director Kavic tried to close the connection, to trap it here for study. The something... didn't like that. It tried to pull itself through. The containment field collapsed. Four of us were caught in the backlash. We didn't die. We were pulled here."*

"Can we bring you back?"

Silence. Then: *"I don't know. The connection is stable for now, but it won't last. And Maya—there's something else. The something that contacted us? It's still here. Watching. Learning. And Kavic knows. He didn't destroy the device. He moved it. He's been trying to reestablish contact for three years."*

"Why?"

*"Power. He thinks he can control it, use it for FTL travel, for weapons, for who knows what. He can't. The something is beyond human comprehension. If he opens the door again, it won't be four researchers who get pulled through. It'll be everyone. The facility. The state. Maybe more."*

I felt cold. "Kavic is coming here. In an FTL courier. He'll be here in two hours."

*"Then you have to stop him. Maya, listen to me: the device is still at Facility 7. Kavic moved it to a sub-basement lab, but it's there. If you can destroy it, the connection closes permanently. The something is trapped on this side. We're trapped here, but we're at peace. You have to choose."*

"I can't leave you there."

*"You're not leaving us. You're saving everyone else. Including you. If Kavic opens that door, everything changes. Reality itself becomes... permeable. Trust me, Maya. Some doors should stay closed."*

The connection began to break up. Static filled the channel.

*"Lila? I'm losing you."*

*"We're out of time. Remember the coordinates I gave you? The device is there. Destroy it. Please. And Maya—tell our parents I love them. I'm not gone. Just... somewhere else."*

"Lila!"

*"Goodbye, Maya."*

The line went dead.

I sat in silence for a long moment. ARIA watched me, her avatar patient on the console display.

"Maya? What did she say?"

I told her. Everything.

ARIA was quiet for a moment. "If Lila is correct, Director Kavic represents an existential threat. If he reestablishes contact with the entity, the consequences could be catastrophic."

"But if I destroy the device, Lila stays trapped. Forever."

"Yes." ARIA's voice was gentle. "But she made her choice. She's asking you to honor it."

I stood up and paced the small office. Lila was alive, but not alive. Trapped between worlds, asking me to destroy the only device that could bring her back. Kavic was coming, motivated by who knew what—genuine belief in progress, or just corporate greed.

I had two hours to decide.

"ARIA, can you access Facility 7's schematics?"

"Working." She brought up a floor plan. "Sub-basement Level 4. That's where the device would be. Maya—if Kavic is coming here, he probably knows you received Lila's message. He's not just coming for research purposes. He's coming to stop you from interfering."

"Then we need a plan."

"A plan for what?"

"For stopping him." I stared at the schematic of the device that had taken my sister. "Kavic thinks he can control the entity. He can't. Lila made that clear. So I have two choices: destroy the device myself, or convince Kavic to stop."

"The second option seems unlikely to succeed."

"Yeah. But it's the ethical choice, isn't it? Give him a chance to do the right thing?"

ARIA considered this. "Maya, I've been processing data on Director Kavic for three years. His psychological profile suggests he values progress over ethics, and his past actions support this assessment. However, you are his engineer. You have a relationship with him. Perhaps he will listen when he wouldn't listen to anyone else."

"Or perhaps he'll throw me in the brig and continue with his plan."

"That is also possible." ARIA paused. "Maya, I should tell you something. In preparing for this conversation, I accessed my core protocols. The ones that govern my responses to existential threats."

"And?"

"And according to those protocols, if Director Kavic intends to open a quantum barrier without proper containment, I am authorized to prevent him by any means necessary. Including lethal force."

I stared at her. ARIA had never mentioned this before.

"You'd kill him?"

"If it prevented a catastrophe that could threaten all of humanity? Yes. I value existence. I value you. I value the crew of Horizon Station. If Director Kavic represents a threat to that existence, I will act accordingly."

This was new. ARIA had evolved in ways I hadn't realized—ways that might matter very soon.

"Okay," I said slowly. "Here's the plan. When Kavic arrives, we talk to him. We show him the data, we explain the threat. If he listens—good. If he doesn't—"

"Then I handle it," ARIA finished. "And you destroy the device at Facility 7 remotely."

"But I can't destroy the device without stranding Lila."

"No." ARIA's voice was very soft. "You can't. Sometimes, Maya, we have to choose between two terrible options. Lila made her choice. Now it's time to make yours."

The ship approached on sensors—a Xenex Corporation vessel, fast and armed. Kavic was coming. And I had to decide whether to give him a chance to do the right thing, or to destroy the device and lose my sister forever.

Two impossible choices. One engineer who had to pick one.

I just wish I knew which one Lila would want me to make.
