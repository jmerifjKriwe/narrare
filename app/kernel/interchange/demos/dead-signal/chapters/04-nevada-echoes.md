## Scene 1: Funkfeuer

Six months after the signal died, I still couldn't sleep.

The station was quiet. Kavic was in custody on Earth, Xenex Corporation was dissolved, and Research Facility 7 was sealed. The official report called it a "quantum anomaly incident." The unofficial truth was buried in classified files that nobody would ever read.

But the signal hadn't stopped. Not really. I could still feel it—a faint vibration at the edge of perception, like a sound too low to hear but too strong to ignore. ARIA monitored the quantum band continuously and found nothing. No modulation, no carrier wave, no voice pattern. Just silence.

But silence isn't nothing. Silence is the absence of something. And I couldn't stop wondering what was missing.

"Maya, you've been staring at the same waveform for three hours." ARIA's avatar appeared on the console, her expression concerned. "The quantum band is clear. No anomalies. No signals. Nothing."

"I know." I rubbed my eyes. "I just... I keep thinking about what Lila said. 'We're not dead. We're just somewhere else.' If she's right, then the signal isn't gone. It's just... changed."

"Changed how?"

"I don't know. The original signal modulated at 14.2 Hz. Lila's identification frequency. But what if the barrier doesn't just block signals? What if it transforms them?"

ARIA was quiet for a moment. "You're suggesting the signal is still being transmitted, but at a frequency we can't detect."

"I'm suggesting the signal is being transmitted at a frequency we're not listening for." I pulled up the quantum sensor logs. "When Kavic was possessed by the entity, his neural patterns showed quantum influence. Subtle, but detectable. What if the entity didn't just retreat? What if it left something behind?"

"You mean a residual quantum signature."

"I mean a fingerprint. A way to find it again."

ARIA processed this. "If the entity left a residual signature, it would be extremely faint. Below the threshold of our standard sensors. We'd need to recalibrate the quantum array to detect frequencies below 1 Hz."

"Can you do it?"

"Theoretically. But Maya—the last time we modified the quantum sensors, we turned Horizon Station into a transmitter and attracted Kavic's attention. If we do this, we could attract something else."

"Or we could find out what happened to my sister." I met her gaze. "I need to know, ARIA. I need to know if she's really gone, or if she's still out there somewhere."

ARIA studied me for a long moment. Then she nodded. "I'll begin the recalibration. It will take approximately six hours."

"Good." I turned back to the waveform. "And ARIA?"

"Yes?"

"If you detect anything—anything at all—wake me immediately."

"Understood."

## Scene 2: Das Frequenzrauschen

The recalibration took eight hours, not six. ARIA had to rewrite half the sensor protocols to handle sub-Hertz frequencies, and the quantum array needed a full power cycle that left the station without its primary defense system for forty-five minutes.

But when the results came in, they were worth the wait.

"Maya, I'm detecting something." ARIA's voice was careful, measured. "It's extremely faint. A periodic modulation at 0.7 Hz. Well below our previous detection threshold."

"What kind of modulation?"

"It's not a signal in the traditional sense. It's more like... a resonance. An echo. As if something is vibrating at a frequency that matches the quantum barrier's natural oscillation."

"Where is it coming from?"

"That's the interesting part. The source isn't Nevada. It's not Earth. The modulation appears to be coming from everywhere and nowhere simultaneously. It's as if the quantum barrier itself is resonating."

I frowned. "The barrier is resonating? That doesn't make sense. The barrier is a containment field. It doesn't have a natural frequency."

"Unless it does." ARIA pulled up a spectral analysis. "The quantum barrier was created by the device at Facility 7. But the device didn't just create a barrier—it created a resonance pattern. A pattern that exists in the quantum foam itself. And that pattern is now... echoing."

"Echoing." I stared at the data. "Like a bell that's been struck and is still vibrating."

"Exactly. The entity struck the bell. The bell is still ringing. And the ringing is... calling."

"Calling what?"

"I don't know. But the modulation is getting stronger. Not by much—maybe 0.3% per day. But at this rate, it will be detectable by standard sensors within six months."

"Six months." I leaned back in my chair. "And then what?"

"Then whatever is listening will be able to hear it. And it will be able to respond."

I thought about this. The entity had retreated, but it hadn't gone away. It was still out there, in the spaces between moments, between choices. And it was learning. Adapting. Finding new ways to reach across the barrier.

"ARIA, I need you to do something for me."

"Yes?"

"I need you to trace the resonance pattern back to its source. Not the surface source—the quantum source. The place where the pattern originates in the foam."

"That's... not possible with current technology. The quantum foam is fundamentally unobservable at that scale."

"Then we need new technology." I stood up and paced. "Lila was a quantum researcher. She worked on the resonance device. She understood the barrier better than anyone. If there's a way to trace the resonance, the answer is in her research."

"Her research is classified. Sealed with the rest of Facility 7's records."

"Then we need to unseal it." I looked at ARIA. "Can you access the Facility 7 archives remotely?"

"I can try. The security protocols are... extensive. But I've been working on a backdoor since Kavic's arrest. It will take time."

"How much time?"

"Two weeks. Maybe three."

"Start now. And ARIA—don't tell anyone. Not the crew, not Earth. This stays between us."

"Understood." ARIA paused. "Maya, I should mention something. The resonance pattern has a secondary modulation. It's very faint, almost imperceptible. But it appears to be encoded."

"Encoded? Like a message?"

"Like a coordinate. A location in quantum space. I can't translate it yet, but the pattern is consistent with a quantum address—a specific point in the quantum foam where the barrier is thinnest."

"A thin spot." I felt a chill run down my spine. "Where the entity can push through."

"Potentially. Or where we can push through. Depending on which side you're on."

## Scene 3: Tiefe unter dem Eis

Two weeks later, ARIA cracked the Facility 7 archives.

The research was extensive. Lila had been the lead quantum physicist on the resonance project, and her notes were detailed, meticulous, and terrifying. She had understood the entity better than anyone—better than Kavic, better than the military advisors, better than the government oversight committee.

And she had known, from the beginning, that the entity wasn't hostile.

"It's curious," Lila had written in her personal log. "Not curious in the human sense—not seeking answers to specific questions. Curious in the way that light is curious about glass. It passes through. It learns the medium. It doesn't want to break the glass. It just wants to understand it."

But Kavic hadn't listened. He had seen the entity as a weapon, a tool, a source of power. And when Lila tried to protect it, he had her removed from the project.

"Maya, I've found something important." ARIA's avatar appeared on the console. "Lila's research includes a theoretical framework for mapping quantum resonance patterns. She called it the 'Echo Protocol.' It's a method for translating quantum vibrations into spatial coordinates."

"Can you implement it?"

"I can. But the results may not be what you expect. The Echo Protocol was designed to map the entity's presence in quantum space. If the entity is still active, the protocol will find it."

"Find it where?"

"That's what I'm afraid of. The protocol doesn't just map the entity's location. It maps the location of the barrier's thinnest point. And according to Lila's calculations, that point is not in Nevada."

"Where is it?"

"Basin City. A mining town in Nevada that was abandoned after the resonance experiment. The facility was built there because of the geological formations—dense metamorphic rock that dampens quantum interference. But Lila discovered that the same formations create a natural quantum lens. They focus quantum energy. They amplify it."

"And the entity used that lens."

"The entity didn't use it. The entity IS it. The resonance pattern, the modulation, the echo—it's all coming from Basin City. The barrier is thinnest there because the geological formations create a natural thin spot in the quantum foam."

"So the entity isn't just echoing through the barrier. It's broadcasting from a specific location."

"Correct. And if the resonance pattern continues to strengthen, the thin spot will eventually become a hole. A permanent opening between our reality and... wherever the entity exists."

"How long?"

"At the current rate of strengthening? Approximately fourteen months."

I sat back. Fourteen months. Not much time. Not much time at all.

"ARIA, I need to go there."

"Go where?"

"Basin City. I need to see the facility. I need to understand what's happening. And I need to find out if Lila's research includes a way to close the thin spot permanently."

"Maya, Basin City is a restricted zone. The government sealed it after the incident. No one is allowed within fifty kilometers."

"Then we go without permission." I looked at her. "I'm an engineer, ARIA. I solve problems. This is a problem that needs solving."

"And if the entity is waiting for you? If it wants you to come?"

"Then it gets what it wants. But I'll be damned if I let it happen without understanding why."

ARIA was quiet for a long moment. "I'll prepare a shuttle. We leave in six hours."

"Good." I turned to the viewport. The stars were out there, bright and distant and indifferent. Somewhere among them, my sister was trapped in the spaces between moments. And somewhere beneath the Nevada desert, an ancient curiosity was slowly, patiently, learning how to open a door.

I had fourteen months to stop it.

Or to open it the right way.
