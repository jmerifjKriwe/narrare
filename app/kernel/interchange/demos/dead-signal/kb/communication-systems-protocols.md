# Communication Systems & Protocols

How humanity communicates across interstellar distances.

## Standard Communication

**FTL Courier Ships**: The most reliable method. Ships carry physical data between star systems. Not real-time, but consistent.

**Relay Network**: A network of buoys and repeaters extends through human space. Messages hop from relay to relay, taking weeks or months to reach distant destinations.

**Quantum Entanglement**: Experimental technology. Allows instantaneous communication, but only between paired entangled devices. Fragile over long distances—entanglement degrades with distance.

**Horizon Station Status**: The station has a quantum entanglement link to Earth, but it's degraded over 12 light-years. Maya can send burst transmissions—short data packets that arrive in hours. Real-time communication isn't possible.

## Emergency Protocols

**Mayday**: Automated distress signal that broadcasts continuously. All ships are required to respond to mayday signals within their range.

**Priority Code**: Specific codes for different emergency types (medical, technical, cataclysm). Horizon Station's code is HOR-CRIT-001.

**Silence Protocol**: In case of hostile encounter or contamination, stations can go silent—shutting down all active emissions. ARIA can implement this in seconds.

## The Signal

When Maya receives the signal, it violates every protocol she knows:

**Instantaneous**: No transmission delay at 12 light-years. Physically impossible.

**Clear**: No degradation, no interference, perfect signal quality. Shouldn't happen.

**Recognizable**: The signal uses a code that Maya recognizes—a variant of the quantum resonance protocols that Research Facility 7 was developing. A code that shouldn't exist anymore.

**The Content**: The signal carries a message. Not just data—a voice. Lila's voice, saying things she couldn't know, things that make no sense unless...

Unless she's not dead. Unless she's somewhere else, somewhere that shouldn't be accessible, reaching across a barrier that shouldn't exist.

## Analysis

ARIA runs every diagnostic, checks every system, confirms the impossible a dozen times:

**The signal is real.**

**It's coming from Earth coordinates.**

**From a facility that was destroyed three years ago.**

**From someone who died in that destruction.**

The conclusion is inescapable: either physics is wrong, or reality is wrong, or both. And Maya has to figure out which.

## Role in Story

The communication system represents how we connect with each other across impossible distances. Maya's isolation at Horizon Station is only bearable because she can theoretically reach Earth. The signal breaks that connection—it's not just a message; it's a violation of the rules that make Maya's world make sense.

The signal also represents connection beyond death. If Lila can reach Maya from wherever she is, what does that mean about death, consciousness, and the boundaries between worlds? These questions drive the story's exploration of what it means to be human, to be alive, to be real.
