# Dr. Maya Chen

Chief engineer of Horizon Station, a remote research outpost at the edge of explored space.

## Background

Maya grew up in the orbital colonies around Mars, the daughter of terraforming engineers. From childhood, she was surrounded by technology that made life possible where it shouldn't be. She earned her doctorate in Quantum Communications at 24 and was recruited for the Horizon Station project at 28. For six years, she's kept the station running with ingenuity, stubbornness, and a supply of caffeine that would kill a smaller person.

## Personality

Maya is practical to a fault. She doesn't believe in ghosts, anomalies, or things that can't be measured with instruments. When something breaks, she fixes it. When something doesn't make sense, she gathers data until it does. Her crew jokes that she's part machine herself—a claim she doesn't entirely deny.

But beneath the practical exterior is someone who chose isolation at the edge of space for a reason. Earth holds memories she'd rather not revisit. The station is her refuge, her purpose, and her family all at once.

## Technical Skills

**Specialization**: Quantum communication systems, ftl drive maintenance, emergency repairs under impossible conditions.

**Notable Achievement**: Once repaired a critical reactor breach using a modified tricorder, a roll of duct tape, and pure stubbornness. The engineering team still talks about it.

**Reputation**: Known for solving problems that other engineers declared impossible. Also known for speaking to machines as if they can understand her (she swears they respond better).

## Relationships

- **ARIA**: The station's AI. Their relationship is complicated—more partnership than human-machine interface. Maya talks to ARIA like a person. ARIA responds like one.

- **The Crew**: Maya is responsible for twelve people. She takes this responsibility seriously. They're not just colleagues; they're family.

- **Earth**: She left behind a life there. A family. A reason she doesn't talk about it.

## Secrets

Maya isn't on Horizon Station by accident. She requested the most remote posting available after her younger sister disappeared during an Earth-based experiment with quantum resonance. Officially, it was an accident. Maya never believed it.

## Role in Story

Maya is our viewpoint character—the grounded, practical engineer forced to confront something that shouldn't exist. Her journey is about learning that some things can't be measured or fixed, only accepted. The signal from Earth forces her to confront not just the impossibility of what she's receiving, but the memories she's been running from.

## Voice

First-person POV. Technical but accessible. Pragmatic. Skeptical but ultimately open to evidence, however impossible.
