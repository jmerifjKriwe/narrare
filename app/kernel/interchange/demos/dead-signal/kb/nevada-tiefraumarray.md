# Nevada Tiefraumarray

An underground research facility built into the Nevada Thrust geological formation. The site of the original quantum resonance experiment that created the barrier and trapped Dr. Lila Chen and three other researchers.

## Description

The facility extends fifteen stories deep into the metamorphic rock of the Nevada Thrust. It was built there because the dense, high-pressure geological structure naturally dampens electromagnetic and quantum interference, providing a stable environment for quantum experiments. The facility's quantum array—a ring of crystalline structures—was designed to create and study quantum resonance patterns.

## History

The facility was operational for six years before the incident. During that time, the research team made significant advances in quantum communication and resonance theory. The breakthrough came when they successfully made contact with an entity existing in the quantum foam beyond normal spacetime.

The entity was curious, not hostile. It wanted to understand our world. But Director Kavic attempted to contain it, to trap it for study. The containment failed. The quantum barrier collapsed, and four researchers—including Dr. Lila Chen—were pulled into the space between moments.

## Current Status

The facility was sealed after the incident. Three years later, the thin spot in the quantum barrier began to weaken, and Maya Chen returned to investigate. She activated the quantum regulators, closing the thin spot and establishing a permanent quantum channel—a window between worlds.

The facility remains sealed, but the quantum channel persists in the Nevada Thrust formation, distributed across hundreds of kilometers of crystalline rock.
