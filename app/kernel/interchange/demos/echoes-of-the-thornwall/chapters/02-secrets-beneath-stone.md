title: Secrets Beneath Stone
position: 2

The Market District buzzed with morning commerce, but Elara barely noticed. The compass in her pocket pulsed with a rhythm that only she could feel, pulling her toward a narrow alley between a baker and a chandler.

*"The Curious Squirrel,"* Kaelen had whispered during morning meal. *"If you need things the Order won't provide."* He hadn't asked why she needed them. He never asked anymore.

The shop's bell chimed as she entered. Shelves crammed with oddities—dried herbs, strange minerals, items of uncertain origin—crowded the space. Behind the counter, a woman with sharp brown eyes and dark hair cropped short looked up from her ledger.

"Initiate." The woman's voice was neutral. "The Order usually sends enforcers, not students."

"I'm not here as an initiate." Elara's heart pounded. This was dangerous. Forbidden. *"I need information."*

The woman—Sera, according to the sign—leaned back. "About?"

"About this." Elara showed the compass.

Sera's expression didn't change, but her eyes sharpened. She stepped around the counter, flipped the sign to CLOSED, and gestured Elara toward a back room.

The back room held a table, two chairs, and a door that probably led to a basement. Sera sat, and Elara followed.

"Where did you get this?" Sera asked.

"Thornwall." Elara didn't elaborate.

Sera exhaled slowly. "Do you know what it is?"

"A compass."

"It's a void compass." Sera's voice dropped. "One of three. They were created five hundred years ago by the Temple of Silent Flames. The Order says they were destroyed."

Elara's mind raced. Temple of Silent Flames. She'd never heard of it. *"What does it do?"*

"Points to void magic. Among other things." Sera studied her. "You're void-sensitive. That's why it responded to you."

Elara didn't deny it. Denial would be pointless now.

"The Order hunts void mages," Sera continued. "Some of us—people who know the truth—we help them. When we can." She paused significantly. *"There's a group. People who think the Order's methods are wrong. We call ourselves the Ashen Covenant."*

Elara's breath caught. A rebel group. She'd heard rumors—whispers in the fortress, warnings from masters—but never confirmation.

"I'm not looking for a rebellion," she said carefully. "I'm looking for answers."

"Answers about what?"

"About Thornwall." Elara met Sera's eyes. *"About what really happened forty years ago."*

Sera's expression changed. Something like respect, or recognition. *"The Order says it was an accident. Void research gone wrong."*

"But?" Elara pressed.

"But the Covenant has sources. Sources who say otherwise." Sera stood, moved to a bookshelf, and extracted a worn volume. She opened it to a marked page and showed Elara.

The page showed a diagram of the dimensional portal that had been at Thornwall. Someone had added annotations in a different hand—notes about stabilizers being deliberately tampered with.

*"Sabotage,"* Elara whispered. The compass pulsed against her chest, confirming what she'd already sensed in the ruins.

"There's more." Sera turned more pages. "Archmage Solara's research wasn't about destruction. It was about healing, communication, travel. The Order's founders killed her to seize power and justify their control."

Elara felt sick. Everything she'd been raised to believe—everything the Order stood for—was built on a lie.

*"Why tell me?"* she asked.

Sera's expression softened. *"Because the compass chose you. Because you're asking questions. And because"—she glanced toward the front of the shop—"because someone I trust said you could be an ally."*

Kaelen. He'd known. He'd sent her here, knowing she'd learn the truth.

*"What do I do now?"* Elara asked quietly.

Sera's answer was simple. *"What you've been doing all along. Learn. Ask questions. And when the time comes—choose."*

Elara left the shop with more questions than answers. But she also had something else: knowledge that she wasn't crazy. The compass wasn't just an artifact—it was a key. And somewhere, hidden beneath layers of Order propaganda and suppression, was a door it was meant to open.

Back at the fortress, High Chancellor Morven's cold eyes followed her from the shadows. Elara pretended not to notice. But she knew.

She wasn't the only one watching.

---

*That night, Elara dreamed of Thornwall as it had been. Mages working in collaboration, laughter, hope. And then a man with cold blue eyes—Morven, younger but unmistakable—tampering with stabilizers while no one watched.*

*She woke knowing two things:*

*First: She could never unknow the truth.*

*Second: She needed to get into the Secret Archives before Morven realized she knew enough to be dangerous.*
