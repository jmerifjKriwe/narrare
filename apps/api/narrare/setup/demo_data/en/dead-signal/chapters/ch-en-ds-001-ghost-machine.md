I was in the middle of recalibrating the quantum sensors—again—when ARIA's voice cut through the hum of the engineering deck.

"Maya. Incoming transmission on the emergency frequency."

I didn't look up from the console. " Probably another false positive from the coronal mass ejection detector. That thing's been jumpy since the flare last week."

"Negative. This is... different."

ARIA's tone made me pause. She didn't sound like an AI. She sounded like she'd seen a ghost, which was ridiculous because ARIA couldn't see ghosts and neither could I. We were engineers. We dealt in facts, in things we could measure and fix.

"What kind of different?"

"Instantaneous. No light-speed delay. No signal degradation. And..." ARIA paused, which she never did. "The carrier frequency is using a code that shouldn't exist anymore."

I turned around. "Show me."

The main display flickered with a waveform—beautiful, clean, and impossible. We were 12 light-years from Earth. Any signal should take years to arrive, even with FTL couriers. This signal had arrived instantly.

"That's not possible," I said, because it was the only rational response.

"Correct." ARIA brought up the analysis. "According to all known physics, this signal cannot exist. According to our sensors, it's here. One of these statements is wrong."

"Source?"

"Earth coordinates. Specifically..." ARIA hesitated again. "Nevada. Research Facility 7."

My stomach dropped. "That facility was destroyed three years ago."

"Officially, yes. But the signal is coming from there. And Maya—there's a voice pattern embedded in the carrier wave. It's a match for Dr. Lila Chen. Probability of false positive: zero point zero zero one percent."

Lila. My sister. Dead for three years. Sending me a message from beyond the grave.

"Play it."

The audio crackled through the deck speakers. My sister's voice—clearer than it had any right to be, speaking words that made no sense.

*"Maya. If you're receiving this, the barrier is stable enough. I'm still here. We all are. But the connection is fading, and there's something I need you to know. Director Kavic lied. The experiment didn't fail. It succeeded too well. We're not dead, Maya. We're just... somewhere else. And Kavic's trying to reopen the door. Don't let him. Please."*

Silence filled the engineering deck. I gripped the console until my knuckles turned white.

"ARIA?"

"Yes, Maya?"

"Run every diagnostic. Check every sensor. Tell me this is a glitch."

"I've run diagnostics seventeen times since the signal arrived. The signal is real. Lila Chen's voice pattern matches archived recordings with 99.7% accuracy. The message is not a glitch."

I sank into the nearest chair. Lila was alive. Somewhere. In a way that defied physics, but alive.

"ARIA, can we send a reply?"

The AI was quiet for a moment. "Theoretically, yes. If we reverse the carrier frequency and boost the quantum sensors, we could establish a two-way link. But Maya—there are implications. If this signal is real, then our understanding of quantum mechanics is incomplete. If we open this link, we don't know what might come through."

"I don't care." I stood up. "My sister is out there. I'm not leaving her there."

ARIA didn't argue. She began the modifications to the communication array. But as she worked, she said something that made my blood run cold.

"Maya, I'm detecting a second signal. FTL courier ship. On an intercept course with Horizon Station. Estimated arrival: six hours. It's a Xenex Corporation vessel."

"Kavic." I swore. "He knows about the signal. And he's coming here."

"Probably. Director Kavic has been monitoring this sector for quantum anomalies for three years. He may have been waiting for exactly this kind of event."

I stared at the waveform on the display—my sister's voice, frozen in digital form. Kavic was coming. Lila was reaching out from somewhere beyond physics. And I was stuck in the middle, an engineer who dealt in facts suddenly confronted with a mystery that defied every fact she'd ever known.

"ARIA?"

"Yes, Maya?"

"How long until we can send a reply?"

"Two hours. Maybe less."

"Then we'd better work fast. Because in six hours, Kavic arrives. And somehow, I don't think he's here to help."

---

*I spent the next two hours working with a focus I'd never summoned before. ARIA and I modified the quantum sensors, boosted the power output, and prepared to send a message across 12 light-years and the barrier between life and death. I didn't know if it would work. I didn't know if I believed in what I was doing. But I knew I couldn't not try.*

*Lila was out there. And I was going to bring her home.*
