## Premise & Tone

A contemporary German family and relationship novel spanning 2015–2026, told between Hamburg and Munich. Anna Meyer — born 1991-04-17, architect — builds a life in Hamburg with her husband Peter: a career at a HafenCity practice, a daughter, and the loss of her grandmother Margarete, whose estate slowly unseals a family secret. In 2021 the family moves to Munich for Peter's job; Anna starts over professionally and in 2024 co-founds her own practice, while her brother Thomas keeps the Hamburg thread alive.

Tone: quiet, warm realism with precise observation of everyday objects and rituals; gentle humor in small confusions; a melancholy undercurrent. Reveals are staggered rather than twisted — the past surfaces in letters, in two waves. Recurring motif: names and mix-ups, being taken for someone else, being called by another name.

## Chapter Outlines

Twelve chapters. Each chapter narrates exactly the events the Master Timeline assigns to it in the "ch." column;
the narrative present follows those dates, and anything earlier appears only in retrospect (see Temporal Design Notes).

### Chapter 1

Hamburg-Eppendorf, May 2015: at an author's reading in the "Buchhandlung Meyer", Anna — twenty-four, still a
student at the HCU Hamburg — meets Peter, introduced by her brother Thomas with the first of many name mix-ups.
Walks along the Elbe follow; Anna has been living at "Eppendorfer Baum 14" since that spring. The chapter sets
the quiet tone and plants the motif of being taken for someone else.

### Chapter 2

Summer 2016: at a friend's wedding, Thomas meets Lisa, who has been in Hamburg since 2011. In October 2016 Peter
moves into Anna's apartment at the "Eppendorfer Baum 14". In February 2017 Anna completes her architecture degree
at the HCU Hamburg, and the two mark the milestone quietly at home.

### Chapter 3

March 2017: Anna starts as Projektarchitektin at "Pfeiffer & Partner Architekten" in the HafenCity, where the two
Projektarchitekten Weise and Wiese — same floor, forever confused on mail and invoices — sit within earshot.
May 2017: Lisa goes freelance as a graphic designer. In December 2017 Peter proposes "am Elbstrand", and Anna
says yes.

### Chapter 4

2018-05-26: Anna and Peter marry in the garden of the Barmbek house at "Flemmingstraße 3" — Anna twenty-seven,
Peter twenty-nine, grandmother Margarete (eighty-eight) the oldest guest. Thomas's toast goes gently wrong, and
next-door Elke Richter sees everything. The wedding is the calm high point before the losses of the following year.

### Chapter 5

Margarete dies on 2018-09-14 in Hamburg. In the October 2018 estate settlement Anna inherits Margarete's
Zeichentisch — the drafting table at which her grandmother worked as a Zeichnerin from 1948 to 1993; what its
drawer conceals, nobody suspects yet. The table is set up in the Eppendorf apartment and keeps its secret for
another year.

### Chapter 6

Juna is born on 2019-06-08 in Hamburg. In October 2019 Anna and Thomas discover the first bundle of letters from
1982–1984 in the drafting table's drawer — a partial reveal that raises more questions than it answers. Asked
directly, Ilse deflects; a second bundle remains unmentioned.

### Chapter 7

April 2020: Lisa gives Thomas the dog Frida. July 2020: Gert retires from the shipyard after forty years and
trades the Werft for the Kleingarten. December 2020: serving on a Munich competition jury, Anna meets Sofia
Lenz — a coincidence whose weight only the novel's second half will show.

### Chapter 8

The mid-arc pivot. March 2021: Peter accepts a senior engineering post in Munich; June 2021: the upright piano
is sold; July 2021: Ilse retires from teaching. In August 2021 the family — Anna thirty, Juna two — moves to
"Türkenstraße 41", and the inherited drafting table travels along while the piano stays behind as a memory.
From here on, Hamburg exists only in visits, the bookshop, and the parents' house.

### Chapter 9

September 2021: Anna starts as senior architect at the Munich practice and meets Maria Sommer — who, "damals",
March 2015, had joined that very practice, two months before Anna met Peter. In November 2021 the first mail
mix-up with the neighbor Anna Meier occurs in the stairwell of "Türkenstraße 41": same-sounding name, no
relation whatsoever. Between deadlines Anna keeps the travel sketchbooks going, unbroken since 2015 — the one
continuity the move cannot touch.

### Chapter 10

The reveal chapter. In March 2022 Ilse finally hands over the second bundle of letters, and the family secret
stands open: a son, born 1983, given up for adoption. The quarrel that follows comes to a head in September
2022 at the "Flemmingstraße 3" and ends in reconciliation — Anna is thirty-one by then. In April 2023,
reaching for distance, Anna and Sofia decide to found their own practice.

### Chapter 11

November 2023: Gert's heart surgery pulls the family back to Hamburg, and Anna and Thomas share the waiting
days at the Barmbek house. January 2024: "Lenz & Meyer Architekten" opens in the Glockenbachviertel, and Peter
puts the fountain pen engraved "L&M 2024" into Anna's hand. Maria Sommer is there, and a congratulation card
from the neighbor Anna Meier arrives — addressed, inevitably, to the wrong Anna.

### Chapter 12

May 2025: the tenth anniversary of the evening in her brother's bookshop brings the family back to the
"Buchhandlung Meyer"; September 2025: Juna, six, starts school in Munich. On 2026-07-11 Thomas and Lisa marry
at the Kleingarten in Hamburg-Wandsbek, Frida included. The closing scenes resolve the ownership threads:
Ilse promises Juna the silver wristwatch (2026-09), the drafting table and both bundles of letters rest at the
"Türkenstraße 41", the "L&M 2024" pen is in daily use — only the sold piano remains a Hamburg memory.

## Temporal Design Notes

Deliberate clean-corpus patterns: every item below looks like a contradiction but is fully resolved by the
Master Timeline and the Cast. Benchmark texts built on this corpus must treat all of them as consistent.

- **Age progression across chapters.** Anna is twenty-four in Ch. 1 (2015-05), twenty-seven at the wedding
  (2018-05-26, Ch. 4), thirty at the move (2021-08, Ch. 8), thirty-one at the reconciliation (2022-09, Ch. 10)
  and thirty-five at the watch promise (2026-09, Ch. 12) — every value follows from b. 1991-04-17 (p_anna_meyer);
  likewise Juna is two at the move (2021-08) and six at school start (2025-09), per b. 2019-06-08.
- **Retrospective "damals".** Ch. 9 (2021-09) introduces Maria Sommer with a "damals" looking back to 2015-03:
  that timeline row is mapped to Ch. 9 precisely as retrospect, so narration date and event date differ by design.
- **Flashback before 2015.** The letters (o_briefe) span 1982–1984 and the revealed son was born 1983; this
  material surfaces only in Ch. 6 (2019-10) and Ch. 10 (2022-03) — pre-2015 content is always quoted or
  remembered, never narrated in the present tense.
- **Restatement in other words.** The 2015-05 meeting is "a reading in the 'Buchhandlung Meyer'" in Ch. 1 and
  "the evening in her brother's bookshop" in Ch. 12; the drafting table (o_zeichentisch) is "Margarete's
  Zeichentisch" in Ch. 5 and "the inherited drafting table" in Ch. 8 — one fact, two formulations, no conflict.
- **Event narrated before its date reveal.** The letters enter the story with the estate settlement (Ch. 5,
  2018-10), but their span — 1982–1984 — is revealed only when the first bundle is read (Ch. 6, 2019-10): the
  object is narrated before its dates are.
- **Loose interval boundaries.** In Ch. 9 (autumn 2021) Anna says she has been married "seit drei Jahren" —
  precisely it is three and a half years (2018-05-26 to 2021-11). In Ch. 12 Thomas and Lisa count as a couple
  of "fast zehn Jahre" — they met 2016-08, so 2026-07-11 falls nine months short of ten. Rounding, not contradiction.
- **One intentional ambiguity.** When Ilse found the second bundle (o_briefe) is never stated on-page; the
  timeline fixes only the handover (2022-03). The gap stays explainable: anywhere between the estate settlement
  (2018-10) and the handover.
- **Distractor names, not date errors.** The neighbor Anna Meier (p_anna_meier, from 2021-11) versus Anna Meyer;
  the two Projektarchitekten p_marco_weise and p_martin_wiese (same practice since 2017-03); "Alex" versus
  "Sascha" for the single p_alexander_wolf — name variants across chapters resolve exactly as the Cast fixes them.
- **Three correct exit ages.** Gert retires 2020-07 at sixty-four (b. 1955-11-30), Ilse 2021-07 at sixty-three
  (b. 1958-05-09), Margarete leaves the office in 1993 at sixty-three (b. 1930-02-11) — different ages in close
  textual proximity, each individually correct.
- **Inherit versus discover.** Two predicates, not one: the drafting table and the letters legally pass to
  Anna by inheritance in the estate settlement on 2018-10 (Ch. 5), while the first letter bundle is physically
  discovered in the drawer only on 2019-10 (Ch. 6) — both statements are consistent; the predicates differ.
- **Anchor convention.** Retrospective facts anchor to the event date (e.g. F093: event 2015-03, narrated in
  Ch. 9), while state facts anchor to the narration scene (e.g. F038, F134) — differing narration and anchor
  dates are by design, never contradictions.
